## Verdict: performance is SUFFICIENT and the premise is VALIDATED — proceed to R2.2b wiring

The concurrent regime the wave engine exists for is now **directly benchmarked**, not asserted. Stop
optimizing the isolated probe; the remaining throughput headroom lives in the wiring, where real connection
concurrency provides it for free. Three items must land in R2.2b (below) — they are wiring blockers, not
nice-to-haves.

---

## What was verified (reviewer's own runs)

- **Per-slot gate is correct.** Oracle byte-identical; the C1 occupancy clamp they added passes
  (`wave_engine_clamps_oversized_threads_no_hang`); the depth-K out-of-order test passed **8/8 in isolated
  fresh processes**. (See "one-failure caveat" below — it was a self-inflicted environment artifact, not a
  reproducible race.)
- **Pipelining wins and saturates at depth-4** (reviewer run, 1M rows, T=8192):

  | batch | depth-1 | depth-4 | win | depth-16/64 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 87k | 173k | 1.99× | flat (173k) |
  | 8 | 664k | 1.17M | 1.76× | flat |
  | 32 | 2.14M | 3.10M | 1.44× | flat |
  | 256 | 5.40M | 6.22M | 1.15× | flat |

  Even host-bound, the concurrent wave (3–6M mid/large) decisively beats R1's lpb default (~2.3M end-to-end
  / ~7M exec-layer) and the 156k batcher. The premise holds.

---

## THE headroom finding: depth-4 saturation is HOST-thread-bound, not GPU-bound

The offered rate plateaus at **depth-4** because a **single host thread's submit+harvest loop** is the cap
(3–6M at mid/large batch), while the GPU single-flight drain ceiling is **~30M**. That is a ~5–10× gap that
is pure host-side serialization — one thread cannot keep the ring full.

**The lever is multi-producer host submission** (a concurrent ring with atomic head reservation, many
producer threads). **Do NOT build this as another isolated probe** — real OLTP is many connections, so the
server's connection threads ARE the multi-producers. Realize it as part of R2.2b wiring and measure it
end-to-end. Building synthetic multi-thread concurrency in the probe now would re-create what wiring gives
for free and you'd measure it twice. (Recorded in PLAN §3 "THROUGHPUT HEADROOM".)

---

## Wiring blockers — must land IN R2.2b (the team correctly deferred these; they are now gating)

1. **Enforce the in-flight bound.** Correctness depends on the caller keeping total un-harvested needles
   ≤ `ring_capacity`; `submit_async` clears a wave's status slots before publishing `head`, so an
   over-subscribed circular ring will clobber slots still being read → wrong rows. The engine wiring must
   enforce this (back-pressure / bounded in-flight), not trust the caller.
2. **Host-side harvest deadline.** `harvest`'s spin must carry its own deadline (the kernel's 30s
   `%globaltimer` backstop is the device net; the host needs its own). A stalled wave must surface an error,
   not hang a connection.
3. **Crash / `SIGKILL`-safe shutdown.** A wave-engine process killed without clean `shutdown()` leaves the
   persistent kernel running until the 30s backstop, perturbing other GPU tenants on the `--gpu-reset`-denied
   shared box. This is the original shared-box risk in practice — R2.2b needs a crash-safe story (e.g.
   doorbell in a known location an external reaper can ring, or a short backstop for the engine-owned kernel).

---

## Caveats

- **Absolute numbers may be thermally suppressed in the reviewer's run.** Reviewer's offered-rate at batch
  256 was ~half the committed figures (5.4–6.2M vs 12.9–18.3M), almost certainly thermal from back-to-back
  GPU benchmarks in one session. The *qualitative* result (pipelining wins, depth-4 saturation, host-bound)
  is robust; re-confirm the absolutes on a cool GPU.
- **One-failure caveat (not a blocker).** The depth-K test failed ONCE in the reviewer's session — but that
  run executed immediately after a `timeout`-KILLED wave test whose persistent kernel was still zombied
  (only self-terminates on the 30s backstop). In 8/8 fresh isolated processes it passed. Conclusion: an
  artifact of the killed-run zombie, not a reproducible gate race. It is, however, direct evidence for
  wiring blocker #3 above.

---

## Recommendation / sequence

1. **Proceed to R2.2b wiring.** The probe is sufficient; further isolated tuning has diminishing returns
   (the GPU/protocol is no longer the cap — the single host thread is).
2. **In R2.2b, land the three wiring blockers** (in-flight bound, harvest deadline, crash-safe shutdown)
   and realize the **multi-producer headroom** with real connection concurrency.
3. **Run the END-TO-END offered-rate A/B** (wave vs R1's lpb default, real connections, real result
   delivery + MVCC visibility). That number — not the isolated probe — decides whether wave-in-engine ships.

Until the end-to-end A/B runs, keep R1's launch-per-batch index probe as the shipped default.
